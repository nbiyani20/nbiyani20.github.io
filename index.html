I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Electrical Engineering at Northeastern University, Boston, specializing in Analog, RF, and Mixed-Signal IC Design. My research focuses on analog computing (AI Hardware), power management ICs (PMICs), RF energy harvesting, and energy-detection-based RF architectures for next-generation IoT, biomedical implants, and battery-free wireless systems. Expert in silicon layout, multi-layer PCB, and SI/PI validation.

I work across the full custom IC development flow — from architecture exploration and behavioral modeling to transistor-level design, analog layout, parasitic extraction, post-layout verification, silicon validation, and lab characterization. I am especially interested in circuits that remain efficient, secure, and robust across real-world PVT conditions and manufacturing variations, not just in simulation.

Over the course of my Ph.D, supported by DARPA and NSF funded projects, I have designed and characterized a wide range of CMOS building blocks, including Analog multipliers, RF rectifiers, LDOs, bandgap voltage references, switched-capacitor converters, PLLs, VCOs, operational amplifiers, StrongARM comparators, SAR ADCs, RF filters, and matching and resonator networks for ultra-low-power wireless applications. This work has contributed to publications in JSSC, IEEE ISCAS, and IEEE MWSCAS.

Long-term, I am interested in problems at the intersection of analog/RF IC design and ultra-low-power electronics — building circuits that enable sensing, communication, and energy-autonomous systems to operate securely and efficiently in real-world environments.
